# AAN/AHS 2012 Guideline Update: Migraine Prevention

## Summary

- Identifies medications and supplements with established efficacy for migraine prophylaxis.
- **Level A (Established efficacy)**: Petasites (butterbur) extract 75–150 mg/day.
- **Level B (Probably effective)**: Magnesium (600 mg/day elemental), riboflavin (vitamin B2, 400 mg/day), coenzyme Q10 (100 mg TID).
- **Level C (Possibly effective)**: Feverfew (Tanacetum parthenium).
- Prescription medications with Level A evidence include topiramate, propranolol, and metoprolol.

## Key Nutrient/Supplement References

| Supplement | Evidence Level | Dosing | Notes |
|------------|---------------|--------|-------|
| Butterbur (Petasites) | Level A | 75–150 mg/day | Must be PA-free (pyrrolizidine alkaloid-free) due to hepatotoxicity risk |
| Magnesium | Level B | 600 mg elemental/day | Most effective in patients with low ionized magnesium or aura |
| Riboflavin (B2) | Level B | 400 mg/day | Takes 3+ months for full effect |
| Coenzyme Q10 | Level B | 100 mg TID or 300 mg/day | Some trials show benefit after 3 months |
| Feverfew | Level C | 50–100 mg/day | Variable trial results; may cause mouth ulcers |
| Omega-3 | Insufficient | N/A | Not recommended as monotherapy for migraine prevention |

## Notes

- The 2021 AAN update reaffirmed magnesium, riboflavin, and CoQ10 as options with moderate evidence.
- Magnesium is particularly relevant for patients with menstrually-related migraines and those with low serum magnesium.
- Supplements should be trialed for at least 3 months before judging efficacy.
