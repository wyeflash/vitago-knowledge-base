# ADA Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes—2024: Nutrition Therapy

## Summary

### Medical Nutrition Therapy (MNT)
- MNT is the cornerstone of diabetes management and should be provided by a registered dietitian nutritionist (RDN) as part of the diabetes care team.
- There is no single ideal macronutrient distribution; meal plans should be individualized.

### Carbohydrate Intake
- Carbohydrate counting, glycemic index/load, and carbohydrate quality (whole grains, vegetables, legumes) are useful tools.
- Minimize intake of sugar-sweetened beverages and refined grains.

### Dietary Patterns
- Multiple dietary patterns are acceptable (Mediterranean, DASH, low-carbohydrate, vegetarian, etc.), provided they are nutritionally balanced and sustainable.

### Supplements
- **Vitamin or mineral supplements are not recommended** for people with diabetes without underlying deficiencies.
- **Vitamin D supplementation** is not recommended for glycemic control in people with diabetes (evidence insufficient).
- **Omega-3 supplements** are not recommended for glycemic control, though high-dose EPA may be considered for cardiovascular risk reduction per cardiologist guidance.

### Special Considerations
- **Metformin users**: Monitor vitamin B12 levels periodically; supplement if deficient.
- **Older adults**: Focus on minimizing hypoglycemia risk, maintaining protein intake, and preventing malnutrition.
- **Pregnancy/GDM**: Coordinate with obstetric care; folic acid, iron, and iodine needs are increased.
