# Cochrane Systematic Review: Omega-3 fatty acids for the primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease

## Citation

Abdelhamid AS, Brown TJ, Brainard JS, Biswas P, Thorpe GC, Moore HJ, Deane KH, AlAbdulghafoor FK, Summerbell CD, Worthington HV, Song F, Hooper L. Omega-3 fatty acids for the primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2018 Jul 18;7(7):CD003177. doi: 10.1002/14651858.CD003177.pub3. PMID: 30019766; PMCID: PMC6513557.

## Background

Researchers have suggested that omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids from oily fish (long-chain omega-3 (LCn3), including eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA)), as well as from plants (alpha-linolenic acid (ALA)) benefit cardiovascular health. Guidelines recommend increasing omega-3-rich foods, and sometimes supplementation, but recent trials have not confirmed this.

## Objectives

To assess effects of increased intake of fish- and plant-based omega-3 for all-cause mortality, cardiovascular (CVD) events, adiposity and lipids.

## Search Methods

Searched CENTRAL, MEDLINE and Embase to April 2017, plus ClinicalTrials.gov and WHO ICTRP to September 2016. No language restrictions.

## Selection Criteria

Randomised controlled trials (RCTs) lasting at least 12 months comparing supplementation and/or advice to increase LCn3 or ALA intake versus usual or lower intake.

## Main Results

- **Included trials**: 79 RCTs (112,059 participants)
- **Low risk of bias**: 25 trials
- **Duration**: 12 to 72 months
- **Setting**: Mainly high-income countries, adults at varying cardiovascular risk

### LCn3 (EPA/DHA) supplementation

Meta-analysis suggested **little or no effect** of increasing LCn3 on:

| Outcome | RR (95% CI) | Participants | Trials | Evidence Quality |
|---------|-------------|--------------|--------|------------------|
| All-cause mortality | 0.98 (0.90–1.03) | 92,653 | 39 | High |
| Cardiovascular mortality | 0.95 (0.87–1.03) | 67,772 | 25 | Moderate |
| Cardiovascular events | 0.99 (0.94–1.04) | 90,378 | 38 | High |
| CHD mortality | 0.93 (0.79–1.09) | 73,491 | 21 | Moderate |
| Stroke | 1.06 (0.96–1.16) | 89,358 | 28 | Moderate |
| Arrhythmia | 0.97 (0.90–1.05) | 53,796 | 28 | Moderate |
| CHD events | 0.93 (0.88–0.97)* | 84,301 | 28 | Moderate |

> *Initial analysis suggested reduced CHD events, but this was **not maintained in sensitivity analyses** retaining only low-risk-of-bias trials (effect moved towards null). LCn3 probably makes little or no difference to CHD event risk.

### ALA (plant-based omega-3) supplementation

Increasing ALA intake **probably makes little or no difference** to:

| Outcome | RR (95% CI) | Participants | Trials | Evidence Quality |
|---------|-------------|--------------|--------|------------------|
| All-cause mortality | 1.01 (0.84–1.20) | 19,327 | 5 | Moderate |
| Cardiovascular mortality | 0.96 (0.74–1.25) | 18,619 | 4 | Moderate |
| CHD events | 1.00 (0.80–1.22) | 19,061 | 4 | Low |

ALA **may slightly reduce**:
- Cardiovascular events: RR 0.95 (0.83–1.07), 19,327 participants, 5 RCTs, low-quality evidence
- CHD mortality: RR 0.95 (0.72–1.26), 18,353 participants, 3 RCTs
- Arrhythmia: RR 0.79 (0.57–1.10), 4,837 participants, 1 RCT

### Other findings

- **No dose-response or duration effects** in subgrouping or meta-regression.
- **No evidence** that increasing LCn3 or ALA altered serious adverse events, adiposity, or lipids (though LCn3 slightly reduced triglycerides and increased HDL; ALA probably reduced HDL).
- **Sensitivity analysis** (retaining only low-risk-of-bias trials) moved LCn3 effect sizes towards the null (RR 1.0) for most primary outcomes.
- **Funnel plot asymmetry** suggested missing studies/results would move LCn3 effect sizes towards the null.

## Authors' Conclusions

> This is the most extensive systematic assessment of effects of omega-3 fats on cardiovascular health to date. **Moderate- and high-quality evidence suggests that increasing EPA and DHA has little or no effect on mortality or cardiovascular health** (evidence mainly from supplement trials). Previous suggestions of benefits from EPA and DHA supplements appear to spring from trials with higher risk of bias. Low-quality evidence suggests ALA may slightly reduce CVD event risk, CHD mortality and arrhythmia.

## Implications

- Earlier positive findings were likely driven by **small, high-risk-of-bias trials**.
- The field shifted after this review: subsequent large trials (REDUCE-IT, STRENGTH) used **high-dose prescription EPA** (4 g/day icosapent ethyl), which showed benefit in hypertriglyceridemic patients on statins — a more specific population than the broad supplement trials in this Cochrane review.
- This review covers **supplementation trials**; dietary advice to eat oily fish was less commonly assessed and showed similar null results.
