# Endocrine Society 2013 PCOS Guideline

## Summary

- PCOS is diagnosed by the presence of hyperandrogenism, ovulatory dysfunction, and polycystic ovarian morphology (Rotterdam criteria).
- First-line treatment for metabolic issues is lifestyle modification (diet, exercise, weight loss of 5–10%).
- **Metformin** is recommended for metabolic abnormalities and may restore ovulation in some patients.
- **Myo-inositol** (2–4 g/day): Emerging evidence suggests improvement in insulin sensitivity and ovulation rates; not yet in 2013 guideline but widely studied since.
- **Vitamin D**: Screen and correct deficiency; some RCTs show improvement in metabolic parameters and fertility outcomes.
- **Omega-3**: May improve lipid profiles and inflammation markers in PCOS.
- **Chromium**: Some studies show modest improvement in glucose tolerance; evidence is limited.

## Key Nutrient/Supplement References

| Supplement | Evidence | Recommendation |
|------------|----------|----------------|
| Myo-inositol | Moderate (post-2013) | 2–4 g/day; improves insulin sensitivity and ovulation |
| Vitamin D | Moderate | Screen and supplement if deficient; may improve metabolic parameters |
| Omega-3 | Low-Moderate | 1–2 g/day; may improve TG and inflammatory markers |
| Chromium picolinate | Low | 200–1,000 mcg/day; modest glucose effects |
| NAC (N-acetylcysteine) | Low | May improve insulin resistance and ovulation; similar efficacy to metformin in some trials |
| Berberine | Emerging | May improve insulin sensitivity and lipid profiles; limited high-quality RCTs |

## Notes

- Since the 2013 guideline, substantial evidence has accumulated for myo-inositol, particularly for fertility and metabolic outcomes.
- Vitamin D deficiency is common in PCOS patients (up to 70–85%).
- Inositol is generally well-tolerated; mild GI side effects may occur.
