# AGA Guidelines for Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)

## Summary

- IBD (Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis) involves chronic intestinal inflammation.
- **Vitamin D**: Deficiency is common in IBD patients and associated with disease activity; supplementation to achieve sufficiency (25(OH)D ≥30 ng/mL) is generally recommended.
- **Omega-3 (EPA/DHA)**: Mixed evidence; some trials show modest benefit in maintaining remission in UC, but not consistently in CD.
- **Curcumin**: Some RCTs show benefit as adjunctive therapy in maintaining UC remission; not part of core AGA guidelines.
- **Probiotics**: Specific strains (e.g., VSL#3) have evidence for pouchitis prevention and mild UC; not for Crohn's disease.
- **Iron**: Iron deficiency anemia is common; IV iron often preferred over oral iron due to GI tolerance.
- **Calcium and vitamin D**: Important for patients on corticosteroids to prevent bone loss.

## Key Nutrient/Supplement References

| Supplement | Evidence | Recommendation |
|------------|----------|----------------|
| Vitamin D | Moderate-Strong | Screen and supplement; target ≥30 ng/mL |
| Iron | Strong | Treat deficiency; IV iron preferred if active disease or oral intolerance |
| Calcium | Moderate | 1,000–1,200 mg/day for patients on corticosteroids |
| Omega-3 | Mixed | May have modest benefit in UC maintenance; insufficient for CD |
| Curcumin | Moderate (UC) | Some evidence for UC remission maintenance; consider as adjunct |
| Probiotics (VSL#3) | Moderate | Pouchitis prevention; mild UC adjunct |
| Zinc | Low | Consider if deficient (common in CD with malabsorption) |
| Vitamin B12 | Moderate | CD patients with ileal involvement or resection require monitoring/supplementation |

## Notes

- Folic acid supplementation is important for patients on methotrexate.
- Avoid NSAIDs when possible as they may exacerbate IBD.
- Elemental diet can induce remission in pediatric Crohn's disease.
