# European Guidance for Osteoporosis (2019)

## Summary

- Osteoporosis is defined by BMD T-score ≤-2.5 at the lumbar spine or femoral neck, or by the presence of a fragility fracture.
- FRAX tool is recommended for fracture risk assessment, with adjustments for glucocorticoid use, secondary causes, and lumbar spine BMD when available.
- **Calcium** (1,000–1,200 mg/day) and **vitamin D** (800–2,000 IU/day) are recommended as baseline supplementation for all patients at risk.
- **Vitamin K2 (MK-7)**: Emerging evidence suggests potential benefit for bone mineral density and carboxylation of osteocalcin, but not yet part of core guidelines.
- **Magnesium**: Important cofactor for vitamin D metabolism and bone matrix formation; deficiency should be corrected.
- Pharmacologic therapy (bisphosphonates, denosumab, teriparatide) is recommended for high-risk patients.

## Key Nutrient/Supplement References

| Supplement | Evidence | Recommendation |
|------------|----------|----------------|
| Calcium | Strong | 1,000–1,200 mg/day total intake (diet + supplements) |
| Vitamin D | Strong | 800–2,000 IU/day; target 25(OH)D ≥50 nmol/L (≥20 ng/mL) |
| Vitamin K2 (MK-7) | Moderate (emerging) | Not core recommendation; may be considered adjunctively |
| Magnesium | Moderate | Ensure adequacy (310–320 mg/day for women; 400–420 mg/day for men) |
| Collagen peptides | Emerging | Some RCTs show small BMD improvements; insufficient for recommendation |
| Boron | Insufficient | Not recommended |

## Notes

- Calcium supplementation should be divided into doses ≤500 mg for optimal absorption.
- Very high-dose calcium supplements (>1,500 mg/day) may be associated with increased kidney stone and CV risk; food-first approach preferred.
- Vitamin D deficiency is highly prevalent in older adults, institutionalized individuals, and those with limited sun exposure.
