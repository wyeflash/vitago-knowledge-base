# 2025 AHA/ACC Hypertension Guideline

## Summary

- Defines hypertension as ≥130/80 mmHg (same as 2017 guideline).
- Lifestyle modification is first-line for all patients: DASH diet, sodium restriction (<1,500–2,300 mg/day), weight loss, physical activity, limited alcohol.
- **Potassium**: Increased dietary potassium (3,500–5,000 mg/day) through fruits and vegetables is recommended for blood pressure lowering, especially in the context of high sodium intake.
- **Magnesium**: Some evidence suggests modest BP reduction with supplementation in those with low intake or deficiency; not a core recommendation.
- **Coenzyme Q10**: Some RCTs show small reductions in SBP; evidence is inconsistent and not guideline-endorsed.
- **Beetroot/nitrate**: Dietary nitrates may acutely lower BP; not a sustained recommendation.
- Pharmacologic therapy (thiazide-like diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, CCBs) is indicated based on stage and risk.

## Key Nutrient/Supplement References

| Supplement/Nutrient | Evidence | Recommendation |
|---------------------|----------|----------------|
| Sodium restriction | Strong | <1,500–2,300 mg/day |
| Dietary potassium | Strong | 3,500–5,000 mg/day from foods |
| Magnesium | Low-Moderate | May lower BP if deficient; not core therapy |
| CoQ10 | Low | Inconsistent evidence; not guideline-endorsed |
| Dietary nitrates (beetroot) | Low-Moderate | Acute effect; not sustained recommendation |
| Fish oil/Omega-3 | Low | Not recommended for BP lowering specifically |
| Vitamin D | Insufficient | Not recommended for BP control |

## Notes

- The DASH diet emphasizes fruits, vegetables, whole grains, low-fat dairy, lean protein, and nuts.
- Weight loss of 1 kg is associated with ~1 mmHg reduction in SBP.
- Alcohol should be limited to ≤2 drinks/day for men and ≤1 drink/day for women.
