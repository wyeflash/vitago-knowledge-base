# EASL-EASD-EASO Clinical Practice Guidelines on MASLD (2023)

## Summary

- MASLD (formerly NAFLD) is the most common chronic liver disease globally, strongly associated with metabolic syndrome.
- First-line treatment is lifestyle modification targeting ≥5% weight loss.
- **Vitamin E** (800 IU/day) may be considered in non-diabetic adults with biopsy-proven NASH, based on PIVENS trial data.
- **Omega-3 fatty acids** (EPA/DHA ≥1 g/day) may be used for cardiovascular risk reduction and may have modest benefits on liver fat.
- **Coffee** consumption (2–3 cups/day) is associated with reduced risk of advanced liver fibrosis.
- No specific pharmacologic therapy is universally approved for MASLD itself; treatment focuses on comorbidities (diabetes, obesity, dyslipidemia).

## Key Nutrient/Supplement References

| Supplement | Evidence | Recommendation |
|------------|----------|----------------|
| Vitamin E (alpha-tocopherol) | Moderate (PIVENS) | 800 IU/day in non-diabetic NASH; discuss risks (prostate cancer signal in SELECT trial) |
| Omega-3 (EPA/DHA) | Low-Moderate | ≥1 g/day for CV risk; modest liver fat reduction |
| Coffee | Moderate (observational) | 2–3 cups/day associated with lower fibrosis risk |
| Vitamin D | Insufficient | Not recommended specifically for MASLD treatment |
| Probiotics | Emerging | Some RCTs show modest improvement in liver enzymes; insufficient for recommendation |
| Resveratrol | Insufficient | Not recommended |
| Milk thistle (silymarin) | Low | Not recommended as standard therapy |

## Notes

- Weight loss of ≥10% is associated with NASH resolution and fibrosis regression.
- Bariatric surgery is effective for eligible patients with obesity and MASLD.
