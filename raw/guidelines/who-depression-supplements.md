# WHO Depression and Anxiety: Nutritional and Supplement Considerations

## Summary

- Depression is a leading cause of disability worldwide; anxiety disorders are similarly prevalent.
- Standard treatments include psychotherapy and pharmacotherapy (SSRIs, SNRIs).
- **Omega-3 fatty acids (EPA)**: Meta-analyses suggest EPA ≥60% of total omega-3 at doses of 1–2 g/day may have adjunctive antidepressant effects, especially in those with high inflammatory markers or inadequate omega-3 intake.
- **Vitamin D**: Deficiency is associated with depression; supplementation may help if deficient, but evidence for treating non-deficient patients is mixed.
- **SAMe (S-adenosylmethionine)**: Some RCTs show efficacy comparable to TCAs; may be considered as adjunctive therapy.
- **5-HTP**: Precursor to serotonin; some evidence for mild-to-moderate depression, but quality of studies is variable.
- **Magnesium**: Low intake is associated with depression; supplementation may have modest benefits, particularly in those with low magnesium status.
- **B vitamins (folate, B12, B6)**: Low folate and B12 are associated with depression and poor SSRI response; supplementation may enhance antidepressant response.
- **Zinc**: Some evidence for adjunctive use in depression at 25 mg/day elemental zinc.

## Key Nutrient/Supplement References

| Supplement | Evidence | Notes |
|------------|----------|-------|
| Omega-3 (EPA-rich) | Moderate | 1–2 g/day EPA; best as adjunct to standard therapy |
| Vitamin D | Low-Moderate | Most beneficial if baseline levels are low |
| SAMe | Moderate | 800–1,600 mg/day; may interact with SSRIs (serotonin syndrome risk) |
| 5-HTP | Low-Moderate | 100–300 mg/day; avoid combining with SSRIs without medical supervision |
| Magnesium | Low-Moderate | 200–400 mg/day elemental; glycinate or citrate forms preferred |
| Folate (L-methylfolate) | Moderate | 7.5–15 mg/day may enhance SSRI response in those with MTHFR variants |
| Zinc | Low-Moderate | 25 mg/day elemental as adjunct |
| Probiotics (psychobiotics) | Emerging | Some strains (Lactobacillus, Bifidobacterium) show preliminary promise for mood |

## Notes

- Supplements should NOT replace standard depression/anxiety treatment without medical supervision.
- The gut-brain axis is an emerging area; dietary patterns (Mediterranean diet) show stronger evidence than individual supplements.
- Alcohol and recreational drugs can worsen depression and interact with supplements/medications.
