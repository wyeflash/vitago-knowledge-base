# 2026 ACC/AHA Dyslipidemia Guideline

## Summary

- Emphasizes LDL-C as the primary target for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) risk reduction.
- Statins remain first-line therapy for lowering LDL-C.
- For patients with elevated triglycerides (≥150 mg/dL), icosapent ethyl (highly purified EPA) at 4 g/day is recommended to reduce cardiovascular events in statin-treated patients (based on REDUCE-IT trial).
- Plant sterols/stanols (2 g/day) and soluble fiber (10–25 g/day) may be considered as adjuncts for LDL-C lowering.
- Lifestyle modification (Mediterranean/DASH diet, physical activity, weight management) is foundational.

## Key Nutrient/Supplement References

| Supplement | Evidence | Recommendation |
|------------|----------|----------------|
| Icosapent ethyl (EPA) | Strong (REDUCE-IT) | 4 g/day for TG ≥150 mg/dL on statins |
| Plant sterols/stanols | Moderate | 2 g/day as adjunct |
| Soluble fiber | Moderate | 10–25 g/day |
| Red yeast rice | Limited | Contains monacolin K (lovastatin-like); variable quality |
| Niacin | Neutral/Negative | Not recommended (HPS2-THRIVE, AIM-HIGH negative results) |
| Vitamin D | Insufficient | Not recommended for lipid modification |

## Notes

- Omega-3 supplements containing both EPA and DHA (over-the-counter fish oil) do NOT show the same cardiovascular benefit as prescription icosapent ethyl in REDUCE-IT.
- The 2018 guideline (PMID: 30423391) was the predecessor; the 2026 update refines risk stratification and non-statin therapy sequencing.
