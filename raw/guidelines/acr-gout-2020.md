# 2020 ACR Guideline for the Management of Gout

## Summary

- Gout is caused by hyperuricemia and deposition of monosodium urate crystals.
- Acute gout: NSAIDs, colchicine, or corticosteroids as first-line.
- Long-term urate-lowering therapy (ULT): Allopurinol as first-line; febuxostat with cardiovascular caution.
- Target serum urate: <6 mg/dL (<360 µmol/L), or <5 mg/dL for tophaceous gout.
- **Vitamin C**: Some evidence suggests modest urate-lowering effect (500–1,500 mg/day); ACR conditionally recommends against specifically using vitamin C for urate lowering due to insufficient evidence.
- **Cherry products**: Observational studies and small RCTs suggest reduced gout flares; not formally recommended as replacement for ULT.
- **Omega-3**: No direct recommendation for gout, but may be used for comorbid cardiovascular risk.
- Weight loss and dietary modification (reduce purine-rich foods, alcohol, fructose-sweetened beverages) are strongly recommended.

## Key Nutrient/Supplement References

| Supplement | Evidence | ACR Recommendation |
|------------|----------|-------------------|
| Vitamin C | Low-Moderate | Conditionally recommends against specifically for urate lowering |
| Cherry extract/juice | Low (observational + small RCTs) | Not formally recommended; may be adjunctive |
| Omega-3 | Indirect (CV benefit) | Not for gout specifically |
| Coffee | Moderate (observational) | Not formally recommended; associated with lower gout risk |
| Dairy products | Moderate | Low-fat dairy associated with lower gout risk |
| Vitamin D | Insufficient | Not recommended for gout management |

## Notes

- Alcohol (especially beer and spirits) is a major trigger; limit or avoid.
- High-fructose corn syrup increases uric acid production.
- Purine-rich vegetables are NOT associated with increased gout risk (unlike purine-rich meats and seafood).
