# Vitamin D for preventing acute respiratory infections in children up to five years of age

**Source:** Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2026 Apr 27;4(4):CD015111.
**DOI:** 10.1002/14651858.CD015111.pub2
**PMCID:** PMC13112518
**PMID:** 42037591

## Background
Acute respiratory infections (ARIs) are a leading cause of morbidity and mortality in children under five. Vitamin D deficiency is common amongst pregnant women and young children.

## Main Results
- **107 RCTs** with **31,521 participants**
- Vitamin D vs placebo: may result in a slight reduction in proportion of children making ARI-related healthcare visits (RR 0.95, 95% CI 0.91 to 1.00; low-certainty evidence)
- Probably does not reduce mean number of ARI-related healthcare visits per child (moderate-certainty evidence)
- Higher vs lower doses: probably do not reduce ARI-related healthcare visits
- Hypercalcaemia occurs infrequently; little to no effect on hypercalcaemia risk

## Authors' Conclusions
Low-certainty evidence that vitamin D supplementation during pregnancy or early childhood may result in a slight reduction in ARI-related healthcare visits. Need for large, well-designed placebo-controlled trials to confirm potential benefit.
