# Calcium and vitamin D for increasing bone mineral density in premenopausal women

**Source:** Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2023 Jan 27;1(1):CD012664.
**DOI:** 10.1002/14651858.CD012664.pub2
**PMCID:** PMC9881395
**PMID:** 36705288

## Background
Osteoporosis is a condition where bones become fragile due to low bone density and impaired bone quality. Preventive measurements need to be addressed throughout the life course. Exercise and a healthy diet are among lifestyle factors that can help prevent the disease, including intake of key micronutrients for bone, such as calcium and vitamin D.

## Objectives
To evaluate the benefits and harms of calcium and vitamin D supplementation, alone or in combination, to increase BMD, reduce fractures, and report potential adverse events in healthy premenopausal women compared to placebo.

## Main Results
- **7 RCTs** with **941 participants**
- Calcium vs placebo: may have little to no effect on total hip or lumbar spine BMD (low-certainty evidence)
- Vitamin D vs placebo: no differences in lumbar BMD between groups
- Calcium + vitamin D vs placebo: no difference in total hip or lumbar spine BMD

## Authors' Conclusions
Results do not support the isolated or combined use of calcium and vitamin D supplementation in healthy premenopausal women as a public health intervention to improve BMD. It is unlikely to have a benefit for fracture prevention in this population.
